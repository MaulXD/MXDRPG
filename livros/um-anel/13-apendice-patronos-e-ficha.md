## Appendix (title page, p.211)

Epigraph: *"But great though his lore may be, it must have a source."*

Introductory text (p.212): This appendix contains: **Patrons** (biographical data and rules for six wise/distinguished individuals who can become allies of the Company); **Nameless Things** (rules to give substance to ancient, formidable, unique horrors); **Landmarks** (one of the main ways *The One Ring* presents playable material), including **The Star of the Mist**, a complete example landmark/adventure.

## Patrons (p.212-221)

Epigraph: *"'And now,' said the wizard, turning back to Frodo, 'the decision lies with you. But I will always help you.'"*

As seen on p.52, Player-heroes will encounter Men, Elves, Dwarves, and Hobbits who oppose the Shadow and may become Patrons. A Company typically meets a potential Patron during an Adventuring Phase, but may only designate **one** as their main Patron at a time (exclusive Fellowship Bonus and advantages). Patrons are designated during a Fellowship Phase via the **Meet Patron** Undertaking (p.121). The book presents six of the most influential individuals of the late Third Age who may serve as Patrons.

Each Patron entry below includes: an in-fiction quote, a short biography, an "Encountering [Name]" section (how/where they're typically met), a "[Name] as a Patron" section (their motivations/use as Patron), a stat card (**Name: Role(s)** / **Distinctive Features**), and their **Fellowship Bonus** + named **Advantage**.

### Balin, son of Fundin (p.212-213)

> *"…there was a very old-looking dwarf on the step with a white beard and a scarlet hood; and he too hopped inside as soon as the door was open, just as if he had been invited."*

Born in year 2763 of the Third Age, eldest son of Fundin, of royal blood (descended from King Náin II) and Durin's Folk. A venerable Dwarf who has seen much loss: fought Orcs when his father died defending Moria, was present when Thráin disappeared in Mirkwood, and travelled with Thorin Oakenshield's company on the quest that reclaimed Erebor and slew Smaug. For decades Balin was Thorin's counsellor; now serves Dáin Ironfoot as one of his most trusted officers, though he remains an adventurer at heart and rarely stays long at court.

**BALIN SON OF FUNDIN:** Adventurer, Envoy
**DISTINCTIVE FEATURES:** Eager, Honourable

**Encountering Balin:** Often travelling across Wilderland and Eriador as Dáin's messenger, or with Gandalf. Visits the Shire (dear friend Bilbo Baggins) and works with Dwarves of the Blue Mountains. Unusually woodcrafty for a Dwarf (tracking, fire-making, pathfinding, keen sight). May be found in a small inn between Erebor and the Blue Mountains, or accompany adventurers heading the same way.

**Balin as a Patron:** Genuinely interested in other folk, especially fond of Hobbits; shares Gandalf's concern about the growing Shadow. Aims to restore Dwarven strength and presence in Eriador; hates Orcs and seeks to drive them back. Eager for information on Dwarf-holds fallen to the Enemy, and actively encourages allies to scout/reclaim such places (particularly interested in Moria, where his father died — King Dáin has cautioned against this, though Balin, Ori, and Óin are not easily dissuaded).

**FELLOWSHIP BONUS:** +1
**ADVANTAGE:** *Balin's Counsel* — You can spend Fellowship points to make a combat roll Favoured.

### Bilbo Baggins (p.213-215)

> *"…he looked and behaved exactly like a second edition of his solid and comfortable father, got something a bit queer in his make-up from the Took side, something that only waited for a chance to come out."*

In the year 2965, Bilbo is a vigorous 75-year-old Hobbit, still much as he was at fifty. His unexpected journey with Thorin's company and a Dragon changed him profoundly, giving him a taste for adventure and estranging him from ordinary Shire society. He returned wealthier and now spends his time on his own peculiar lifestyle, the company of friends (often Dwarves), and his private diary/records, which he plans to turn into a real book.

**BILBO BAGGINS:** Retired adventurer, Burglar
**DISTINCTIVE FEATURES:** Fair-spoken, Cunning

**Encountering Bilbo:** Lives at Bag End, Underhill, Hobbiton — likely having tea or smoking a pipe at the door. Sometimes stays away from home longer than proper Shire etiquette allows. If encountered outside the Shire, he might carry his Elf-dagger Sting, though he prefers to vanish (an uncanny talent) rather than fight; always carries a pocket-handkerchief.

**Bilbo as a Patron:** Knows a great deal about the world outside the Shire and its dangers, kept informed via letters and rumour. Happy to arrange hospitality and finance expeditions in exchange for a good story or keepsake; growing isolated from fellow Hobbits, who see him as an eccentric recluse.

**Sidebar — Bilbo's Magic Ring (p.214):** Bilbo found a magic ring in an underground cave under the Misty Mountains and keeps it a great secret. It makes him invisible, useful for avoiding unwelcome callers. He has carried it over twenty years, keeps it on a fine silver chain in his waistcoat pocket, and has developed the habit of touching it to reassure himself it is still there — a growing hold over his mind that he doesn't fully understand. (Flavor/foreshadowing text — no explicit item mechanics given here.)

**FELLOWSHIP BONUS:** +2
**ADVANTAGE:** *Bilbo's Hospitality* — By choosing the Meet Patron undertaking to visit Bilbo you additionally raise the Fellowship rating by +1 until the next Fellowship Phase.

### Círdan the Shipwright (p.215-216)

> *"As they came to the gates Círdan the Shipwright came forth to greet them. Very tall he was, and his beard was long, and he was grey and old, save that his eyes were keen as stars…"*

Perhaps the most ancient Elf still in Middle-earth, having witnessed three ages of the world. Known as the Shipwright and Master of the Grey Havens; a member of the White Council; formerly bore Narya, the Ring of Fire, before giving it to Gandalf the Grey. Chief of all Elves west of the Blue Mountains (Lindon) since Gil-galad's death in the War of the Last Alliance. Considered to possess the gift of great foresight, hence his role preparing the ships that carry Elves from Middle-earth to Valinor.

**CÍRDAN THE SHIPWRIGHT:** Emissary, Lore-master
**DISTINCTIVE FEATURES:** Courteous, Lordly, Wise

**Sidebar — The Elendil Stone (p.216):** Círdan and the Elves of Lindon guard one of the seven palantíri brought to Middle-earth by Elendil, locked away in the tower of Elostirion in the Tower Hills. A perfectly smooth, dark glassy globe about a foot across; palantíri traditionally allow seeing at a distance and communicating with each other. Only a few of the Wise know whether the Elendil Stone still functions, as the other stones are lost or destroyed.

**Encountering Círdan:** Dwells at Mithlond on the Gulf of Lhûn; rarely leaves the Grey Havens, but welcomes all who come in peace. His herald Galdor of the Havens often travels representing him with full authority. In person, very tall even for an Elf, grey-haired with a long silver beard, gracious and hospitable; his eyes hold a deep well of memory.

**Círdan as a Patron:** Knows the Havens are vital to the war against the Shadow; the Grey Havens serve as the last refuge for those leaving Middle-earth. Consults often with Elrond via messengers on the East Road.

**FELLOWSHIP BONUS:** +1
**ADVANTAGE:** *The Shipwright's Foresight* — You can spend Fellowship points to repeat any one roll. By choosing the Meet Patron undertaking to go and see Círdan you additionally receive a rumour from the Loremaster.

### Gandalf the Grey (p.217-218)

> *"At the end of the second week in September a cart came in through Bywater from the direction of the Brandywine Bridge in broad daylight. An old man was driving it all alone. He wore a tall pointed blue hat, a long grey cloak, and a silver scarf."*

To Shire Hobbits, Gandalf is just a wandering pilgrim who claims to be a Wizard, known mainly for fireworks and smoke-rings. Elsewhere he is known as Mithrandir (Elves), Tharkûn (Dwarves), or "Grey Pilgrim"/bringer of ill-news (Men of the South). In truth he is Sauron's greatest enemy in the Third Age. Wizards came to Middle-earth from the Uttermost West nearly two thousand years ago to bolster the hearts of those fighting evil. Gandalf secretly bears Narya, the Ring of Fire (Celebrimbor's third Elven-ring), given to him by Círdan; in combat he wields Glamdring, forged in Gondolin for the Goblin-wars.

**GANDALF THE GREY:** Adventurer, Wizard
**DISTINCTIVE FEATURES:** Bold, Cunning, Wise

**Encountering Gandalf:** Can be met almost anywhere — riding, walking alone or with company, or driving a cart. Holds particular fondness for Shire Hobbits; in Eriador, prefers meeting at the Bree inn. Along the road he may appear as a simple old man with a staff; in council, his white hair and piercing dark eyes recall a wise ancient king. He avoids using magic lightly (it would betray his presence to the Enemy) but displays mastery of fire and smoke; can be quick to laughter, warm reassurance, or sudden wrath at disrespect.

**Gandalf as a Patron:** Círdan once said the Grey Wanderer's mission is to "rekindle hearts in a world that grows chill." Gandalf inspires action and sets events in motion (e.g., helping Thorin reclaim Erebor eliminated a Dragon that could have become a weapon for the Enemy). Unlike Saruman (who deals in devices from his tower), Gandalf moves among those who oppose Sauron directly, listening to pleas and providing help — making him welcome almost everywhere despite a reputation as a troublemaker. A member of the White Council, he is the one most likely to act promptly, infiltrating enemy lairs and facing evil without hesitation, leading by example and encouraging the Free Peoples to set aside old rivalries.

**FELLOWSHIP BONUS:** +2
**ADVANTAGE:** *The Wisdom of the Grey Pilgrim* — You can spend Fellowship points to make a Shadow Test Favoured.

### Gilraen the Fair (p.218-219)

*(One of the book's six official Patrons; listed here in full even though not named in the initial extraction brief.)*

> *"After a few years Gilraen took leave of Elrond and returned to her own people in Eriador, and lived alone…"*

A woman of noble Dúnedain lineage, mother of Aragorn (current chieftain of the Rangers of the North and Heir of Isildur). Widowed young, shortly after Aragorn's birth; Elrond took her and the newborn to Rivendell, raising Aragorn as his own son under the name "Estel" (Sindarin for "Hope") to conceal his lineage. In the year 2965, Gilraen is not yet sixty; being one of the long-lived Dúnedain, she is in the fullness of her strength, possessing a measure of the foresight of her people (inherited from her mother, Ivorwen). When her son left Rivendell to seek adventure, Gilraen chose to remain in Imladris, taking short trips to visit her own people and confer with Ranger captains.

**GILRAEN THE FAIR:** Counselor, Seer
**DISTINCTIVE FEATURES:** Cautious, Fair, Steadfast

**Encountering Gilraen:** Accorded great respect among the Dúnedain of Eriador as Arathorn's widow and as a foreseeing counselor; many Ranger captains seek her advice. Tall and slender, her demeanour marked by sorrow, but she refuses to be bent by misfortune. Can be met with small bands of Rangers near Rivendell, or in the Weather Hills and North Downs.

**Gilraen as a Patron:** Having lost her husband to an Orc-arrow and her father-in-law Arador to Hill-trolls, Gilraen knows the Dúnedain and all of Eriador will never be safe while evil roams unchecked. In her son's absence as chieftain, she makes sure the Rangers do not forsake their duty as guardians of old Arnor, the Bree-land, and the Shire — finding a staunch ally in Gandalf. Adventurers who earn her trust gain the Rangers' help: scouts, directions to hidden refuges, and caches of supplies.

**FELLOWSHIP BONUS:** +1
**ADVANTAGE:** *Gilraen's Folk* — As long as you are within the territory that once was the old realm of Arnor, all Journey Events are determined as if you were in a Border Land. By choosing the Meet Patron undertaking to go and see Gilraen you additionally receive a rumour from the Loremaster.

### Tom Bombadil and Lady Goldberry (p.219-221)

> *"'Tom Bombadil is the Master. No one has ever caught old Tom walking in the forest, wading in the water, leaping on the hill-tops under light and shadow. He has no fear. Tom Bombadil is master.'"*

Old Tom Bombadil once roamed all of Eriador, when the Old Forest reached as far south as Dunland; as the forest shrank, so did his domain. He is a strange, ancient creature — to the Elves, Iarwain Ben-adar ("oldest and fatherless") — older than the long-lived Elves can remember, present before the Dark Lord arrived, having welcomed the first rivers and trees. His life is inextricably tied to his land. He appears as a red-cheeked man with curly brown hair, clad in a blue coat and yellow boots, often engaged in seemingly frivolous activities (like picking water-lilies) — in truth, he simply cares about all living things equally, unable to rank the worth of one life over another. He shares his life with **Goldberry**, the River-woman's daughter, fair as an Elf-maiden and equally enigmatic.

**TOM BOMBADIL:** The Master
**DISTINCTIVE FEATURES:** Absent-minded, Merry

**GOLDBERRY:** River-daughter
**DISTINCTIVE FEATURES:** Fair, Fair-spoken

**Encountering Tom and Goldberry:** Their house lies just beyond the eastern edge of the Old Forest, where the Withywindle runs down from its Barrow-downs spring. Under Tom's roof, guests find contented hearts, peaceful slumber, fine company, joyous song, and simple pleasures. Tom journeys into the Old Forest and the Barrow-downs without fear (not of Old Man Willow nor the Wight-haunted hills), charming foes to sleep with song and dance. Goldberry can be found anywhere along the Withywindle, braiding daisies/water-lilies or singing to the waters; she is friendly to strangers and no less strange to friends; both open their doors to any who meet them.

**Tom and Goldberry as Patrons:** Though Tom's power runs as deep as the earth, it is limited to his little land; within its bounds he is seemingly unconquerable, but he will not step beyond them for any reason. Adventurers who win his and Goldberry's friendship find they appear almost instantly anywhere within their domain when called — even the weather seems to behave to their whims. Tom asks little of his friends directly, but might point them toward a problem needing solving (an unruly Wight from the Barrow-downs, a lost calf in the Old Forest).

**FELLOWSHIP BONUS:** +2
**ADVANTAGE:** *Master of Wood, Water and Hill* — You can spend all your remaining Fellowship to call Tom or Goldberry's intervention anywhere in Tom's country.

## Landmarks (p.221-223) — MECHANIC (system for presenting playable locations)

Epigraph: *"Not far ahead were dreary hills, rising higher and higher, dark with trees. On some of them were old castles with an evil look, as if they had been built by wicked people."*

### There Are Landmarks (p.221)

The map of Eriador is a vast region that seems largely empty, but the land bears traces of a long history: ruins that current denizens use as landmarks for bearings, boundaries, or building material. Many more ruins lie hidden under grass or dirt, unvisited for centuries. Player-heroes learn of Landmarks by gathering rumours (small snippets of legend/hearsay — see Gather Rumours, p.121) during the Fellowship Phase. When the Company decides to explore a Landmark during an Adventuring Phase, the Loremaster uses the Journey rules to get the Company there, then refers to the Landmark's own description to run the exploration.

In brief: Landmarks let players and the Loremaster play open-ended, self-contained scenarios — with described locations, denizens, and events — but they do NOT provide a predetermined plot laid out in orderly scenes. Loremasters can design their own Landmarks using the guidelines below; complete Landmarks are also published in supplements. *The Star in the Mist* (p.223) is an example of a fully-developed Landmark.

### Structure of Landmarks (p.221-222) — the six-part format

All published Landmarks follow this same structure (and Loremasters can use it for their own):

1. **NAME.** Generally, the name that local superstition or legend gives the place (e.g. "Deadmen's Dike," "Norbury of the Kings," "Moria").

2. **RUMOUR.** The piece of information about the place that Player-heroes pick up while adventuring or during the Fellowship Phase. May contain a mix of true and false information, filtered through local prejudices/superstitions.
   - **Old Lore:** Further information available to Player-heroes who research the landmark (while adventuring or during the Fellowship Phase). Generally more precise and truthful than the base Rumour.

3. **BACKGROUND.** An overview of the place for the Loremaster, summarising its main features and why it's interesting or dangerous. Includes general directions on how to get there starting from a known place (e.g. "three days north of Amon Sûl") — but NOT game-terms travel information; for that, the Loremaster uses the Journey rules.
   - **Journey Events:** Information useful to set up potential accidents or other noteworthy events that might occur as the Company approaches the landmark.

4. **MAP.** A drawing illustrating the landmark in as much detail as possible — a map or cutout, always keyed to the descriptions in the Locations section.

5. **LOCATIONS.** A number of paragraphs describing the place in detail, including Treasure information or potential encounters with denizens/Loremaster characters. Some encounters may connect to the Schemes and Trouble section.

6. **SCHEMES AND TROUBLE.** Describes how external forces can interfere with exploration of the site; lists potential self-contained events/accidents that may occur during the Adventuring Phase. Sometimes ties the landmark to a bigger picture (e.g., the schemes of a recurring villain).

**Sidebar — A Note About Rules Presentation in Landmarks (p.222):** Landmark descriptions are kept simple; the text assumes the Loremaster can freely apply the game's normal rules (Journeys for travel to reach it, Skill Endeavours/Risk Levels/Sources of Injury for obstacles the Landmark presents) without the Landmark text spelling this out. As an exception, the sample Landmark that follows (*The Star of the Mist*) provides an additional layer of rules-facing description, presented under "Tutorial" headings, to illustrate the concept for new Loremasters.

## The Star of the Mist (p.223-230) — example Landmark, summarized

*Epigraph: "…the Witch-king came down upon Arthedain before winter was ended."*

A fully worked example Landmark, following the six-part structure above, with extra "Tutorial" boxes explaining Loremaster technique (setting up obstacles, building a climax, expanding a location, meeting NPC factions).

**Rumour/Old Lore:** An ancient tower in the southern Ered Luin foothills, remnant of a castle from the days of the Kings; wanderers speak of an eerie blue light sometimes visible from Sarn Ford. People have started to disappear nearby. Old Lore reveals the tower was built atop a Dwarven ruin, was besieged in the Angmar wars, and fell to treachery — some say Dwarves let the Witch-king's forces in by secret ways.

**Background:** A fortified town once stood here, home to a valorous Dúnedain lord who resisted the Witch-king. The fortress was besieged; the lord was captured and tortured to death before his wife's eyes. The town and castle fell to treachery and were razed, cursed by the Witch-king himself. The noblewoman was consumed by grief and, over the years, became a Fell Wraith; her followers degenerated into horrible, almost deathless creatures. Today only a ruined tower remains, reachable via an underground guard post and a bridge over a deep ravine — currently used as a base by a band of brigands (secretly led by Sabian, a spy from Umbar interested in the ruins of Cardolan).

**Locations:** Include the Brigands' Camp (guarded by Footpads, one per Player-hero), a broken bridge over the ravine, the Dwarven Gate (a guard-post now used as brigand sleeping quarters, hiding a small cache of stolen gold), the Flooded Chambers (a cursed, flooded Dwarven records-hall/Hallows, now home to Marsh-dwellers — twice the number of Player-heroes — who attack any who enter; contains sepulchres, a lesser hoard, and stairs up to the Accursed Halls or into the ruins of the Dwarven city of Narag-nâla, home to a hidden colony of treacherous Dwarves led by the blind Veiko), the Accursed Halls (razed ruins of the town, cursed with Black Speech graffiti — reading it grants 2 Shadow points regardless of comprehension), and the Tower of the Star itself, where the mummified body of Lord Hadirion lies with a cursed crown; removing the crown lifts the curse and finally lays the Fell Wraith Elwen to rest, revealing a greater hoard in a hidden dungeon.

**Adversary stat block — Elwen, the Fell Wraith (p.228-229):**
- **Traits:** Horrific, Sorrowful
- **Attribute Level:** 5
- **Endurance:** 24 | **Might:** 2 | **Hate:** 5 | **Parry:** +2 | **Armour:** 2
- **Combat Proficiencies:** Pitted Blade 3 (4/16, Pierce), Spear 2 (4/14, Pierce)
- **Fell Abilities:** *Denizen of the Dark* (attack rolls Favoured in darkness); *Fear of Fire* (loses 1 Hate/round in close combat vs. a torch/burning-item wielder); *Unliving* (spend 1 Hate to cancel a Wound or damage that would bring her to zero Endurance; immune to Intimidate Foe unless a Magical success is obtained); *Strike Fear* (spend 1 Hate to make all Player-heroes in sight gain 3 Shadow points (Dread); those who fail their Shadow test lose themselves in fog and can't rejoin the group until next round).

**Schemes and Trouble:** Three threads the Loremaster can weave in: **The Brigands** (a band led in truth by the spy Sabian, who will sabotage/trap the party rather than fight openly); **Treacherous Dwarves** (Narag-nâla's Dwarves, descendants of those who betrayed Hadirion, initially hostile but may ally once the Marsh-dwellers/curse are dealt with); **Trapped!** (returning brigands may seal the party inside the ruins from outside).

## Nameless Things (p.231-237) — MECHANIC (system to generate unique, "namable" monsters)

Epigraph: *"There are older and fouler things than Orcs in the deep places of the world."*

The darkest Dwarven legends speak of ancient things living in remote pools, lakes, and dark tunnels — beings older than Morgoth's own creations, unknown even to Sauron, possibly akin to Ungoliant's monstrous breed, or something else entirely. Their origins are unknown; they are evil but do not heed the Shadow's call. All are horrible to behold and very dangerous — not simple beasts, but possessed of primitive intelligence tied to survival instinct, and often some deeper drive or craving. They hate light and live in darkest recesses, lake depths, or underground rivers; they surface only briefly to spread fear before returning to hidden lairs.

### Nameless Things in The One Ring (p.232) — the generation system

A Loremaster looking for a formidable, unique adversary designs a Nameless Thing personally using a set of tables (Feat Die and/or Success Die rolls, or direct choice), producing a creature complete with a level of backstory.

- **Tables 1-5** create the Thing's identity and story:
  - **Table 1** — how the Thing is referred to.
  - **Table 2** — its general description.
  - **Table 3** — what the party notices moments before it appears, and what they see first.
  - **Table 4** — a rumour about the Thing.
  - **Table 5** — where the Thing is remembered/recorded.
- **Tables 6-8** determine its game-terms characteristics:
  - **Table 6** — full adversary stats, including number of Fell Abilities.
  - **Table 7** — its forms of attack.
  - **Table 8** — the full list of Fell Abilities available to it.

All Nameless Things, being creatures of darkness, universally have these two Fell Abilities:
- **Fear of Fire:** The thing loses 1 Hate at the start of each round engaged in close combat with an adversary wielding a torch or other burning item.
- **Hate Sunlight:** The thing loses 1 Hate at the start of each round it is exposed to full sunlight.

### Table 4. A Rumour About the Thing (Feat Die, p.234)

10 flavor-text entries (roll 1 Feat Die) tying the Thing into Middle-earth lore/history, e.g.: (⊘) imprisoned by Sauron at Dol Guldur, freed during the White Council's attack, now a sworn enemy of all living things; (1) disturbed at Lake Evendim by early Men of Arnor, dragged ships and crews into the depths; (2) fought at Dagorlad for Sauron until Gil-galad and Aiglos made it retreat; (3) caused the collapse of Tharbad's bridge after the Great Plague; (4) chased Isildur's squire Othar after Gladden Fields; (5) was unleashed by the Witch-king of Angmar to bring down Fornost's defenses; (6) haunted the Grey Mountains' Dwarven mines, eventually driven off (some say by Thorin himself); (7) was worshipped as a god by Hillmen until driven out by Elves and Men; (8) massacred a Woodmen village on the east side of the Misty Mountains; (9) periodically returns to Mirkwood, causing Spiders to swarm in terror; (10) is greedy for bones, especially craving those of Elves; (⚿) fled Beleriand's ruin and has been moving east ever since.

### Table 5. Where the Thing Is Remembered (Feat Die, p.235)

10 entries describing who/what holds lore about the Thing, e.g.: (⊘) Orcs, who often feed it prey and could be a useful source if one can be made to talk; (1) the Great Eagles, who saw a river-monstrosity vanish without a trace; (2) a rarely-sung Elven song in the Hall of Fire of Rivendell, said to ward it off if sung fully; (3) an inscription in a dark northern cave near the Grey Waste; (4) ancient scrolls in Minas Tirith referencing an evil thing under Mount Mindolluin; (5) a moss-covered burial monument among Northmen of southeastern Mirkwood; (6) a Hobbit nursery rhyme recited on the shore of the Brandywine, about a thing of darkness in the water; (7) Ranger tales of a creature that can appear beside you while sleeping; (8) Saruman's well-stocked (though never fully classified) texts on Middle-earth's dark creatures; (9) the Seeing Stone of Elostirion, which turns to watch only when such an ancient evil approaches the Grey Havens; (10) a parchment in Elrond's private library at Rivendell, with habits and haunts noted; (⚿) only the wisest of Middle-earth know of it — and they fear it, offering only advice on avoidance, never on how to fight it.
