**Setting text:** It is the year 2960 of the Third Age. ## IN THE SHIRE (page 6)

The Shire is described as the introductory, less dangerous setting for new/younger players. The Starter Set uses a simplified rules set and adventuring within limited boundaries; once familiar, players graduate to full *The One Ring* and Eriador.

**The Player-Heroes:** Except for the Loremaster, each player controls a single Player-hero. The Starter Set uses pregenerated, ready-to-play heroes: Hobbits like Drogo Baggins, Paladin Took, Primula Brandybuck, Lobelia Bracegirdle, and more.

**The Loremaster:** Doesn't play a single character but describes the setting, plays Loremaster characters/Adversaries (peaceful or hostile), and adjudicates consequences of the heroes' actions. Begins each session by setting the scene.

**Sidebar — Entering Middle-earth:** The aim is to let players feel what it means to go adventuring in a wild and perilous land more akin to epic sagas or the Dark Ages of Europe than the contemporary world — adopting the viewpoint of individuals whose horizons rarely extend beyond a few miles from their birthplace.

## STRUCTURE OF THE GAME (page 7)

Story-building is fundamental: the game is *created* through play, not just heard. A session is a series of scenes connected in a wider narrative arc, each scene focused on the Company (or part of it) facing/preparing for/recovering from a challenge.

- The Loremaster frames each scene: describes location, who's present (Player-heroes, allies, adversaries), then defines the challenge.
- Players then describe how their characters react.
- Playing out a scene generally only needs the common-sense Action Resolution rules (p.8); combat rules (p.22) are a notable exception, and rare in the Shire.

## ACTION RESOLUTION (pages 8–11)

### When to Roll
The Loremaster asks for a roll only if the action might fail. If there's no doubt about the outcome, the action succeeds automatically. A roll is warranted only when one of these applies:
1. **DANGER** — the action is dangerous (no roll if nothing is risked on failure).
2. **KNOWLEDGE** — the action aims to obtain information not immediately available (no roll if the knowledge isn't secret/hidden).
3. **MANIPULATION** — the action intends to influence one or more uncooperative Loremaster characters (no roll if what's asked is coherent with their motives).

**Sidebar — Scenes in Brief:**
1. The Loremaster describes a situation requiring the players to make decisions.
2. The players investigate the situation, considering options, then describe their action.
3. The Loremaster evaluates the plan and adjudicates whether to "say yes" and grant it, or ask for a die roll.

### The One Ring Dice
A specialised dice set: six 6-sided dice (**Success Dice**) and two 12-sided dice with special icons (**Feat dice**). Included in the Starter Set; extra sets recommended per player.

### Making Rolls
**Who rolls:** Usually the player whose Player-hero is best suited; generally a single player rolls except for group-wide effects.

**Which ability:**
- **Skill Rolls** — the most common type; use one of the 18 Skills.
- **Combat Rolls** — attack rolls use Combat Proficiencies (STRENGTH TN); Protection rolls avoid injury when hit.
- **Valour and Wisdom rolls** — as prompted by fear or sorcery, etc.

### Make the Roll
1. Roll one Feat Die plus a number of Success Dice equal to the rating of the appropriate ability (roll only one Feat Die if the rating is 0).
2. Add all numerical results, compare total to the Target Number (TN) of the relevant ability.
- Total ≥ TN → success; otherwise, failure.

### How to Read the Feat Die
Feat Dice show numbers 1–10, plus two special icons:
- **Gandalf rune (𝍕)** — best possible result; automatic success regardless of TN.
- **Eye of Sauron** — worst possible result; counts as zero.

### How to Read the Success Dice
Success Dice are 6-sided, showing 1/2/3 in outline and 4/5/6 in solid colour; a Success icon (Elvish numeral 1, ᛏ-like glyph, transcribed here as "℧") appears alongside the number 6. Add all Success Dice results to the Feat die result; any ℧ icons rolled indicate a superior result (Degree of Success).

### Target Numbers
All abilities have an associated Target Number.
- Most rolls are made against three **Attribute Target Numbers** (Attribute TNs): STRENGTH, HEART, WITS.
- Each Attribute TN = 20 minus its corresponding Attribute score.

**Example (sidebar):** Paladin Took has AWARENESS rating 2 (a STRENGTH Skill); STRENGTH TN 13. Rolls a Feat die (8) plus two Success dice (4, 5) — total comfortably beats 13, success.

### Repeating a Roll
Normally only one attempt per action. If the Loremaster allows, a failed action can be retried by the same hero using a *different* ability (representing a different approach) — but consequences of the first failure remain.

### Degree of Success
A roll that meets/beats its TN (or rolled the Gandalf rune) and produces one or more Success icons (℧) is of superior quality:
- No ℧ → a **success** (bare minimum).
- One ℧ → a **great success**.
- Two or more ℧ → an **extraordinary success**.

### Die Roll Modifiers

**Favoured Rolls** — a Player-hero has a special affinity for a task: roll two Feat dice, keep the best. Typical sources: Favoured Skills.

**Ill-Favoured Rolls** — opposite; roll two Feat dice, keep the worst. Sources indicated in the Adventures.

**Bonus Success Dice** — a Player-hero may be granted additional Success Dice under favourable circumstances/talents. Bonuses indicated in text as *gain (1d)* or more (add that many Success dice).

**Hope Bonus:** A character's Hope score is a pool representing spiritual vigour. A Player-hero can spend 1 Hope point to *gain (1d)*. Only one Hope point may be spent per roll (can't stack for multiple bonus dice).

**Inspiration:** Certain circumstances make a Player-hero temporarily *Inspired* — Inspired heroes double the benefit of spending a Hope point: gain (2d) instead of (1d).

**Penalty Success Dice** — the opposite of bonuses; a Player-hero suffering a malicious power/spell or unfavourable circumstance may *lose (1d)* or more (down to a minimum of zero Success dice).

**Sidebar — Bonuses vs Penalties:** cumulative — add all gained dice, subtract all lost dice. Example: gains (1d) + gains (2d) − loses (1d) = final gains (2d).

**Sidebar — Favoured vs Ill-Favoured:** If a roll qualifies as both, it's resolved normally (roll one Feat Die only), even if multiple sources would make it Favoured and only one would make it Ill-favoured (or vice versa).

**Sidebar — Ill-Favoured Player-heroes:** Some adversary/special abilities make a Player-hero Ill-favoured on *all* rolls instead of one particular roll. Player-heroes who have spent all their Hope points are considered Ill-favoured on all rolls.

### Conditions
Two conditions recorded on the character sheet:

**WEARY** — reflects Endurance dropping to/below Load score. When a Weary hero makes a roll, all Success Dice showing an outlined number (1, 2, or 3) count as zero instead.

**WOUNDED** — serious injuries. Losing/recovering Endurance is everyday, but being Wounded is more serious and lasts longer. Wounded heroes who remain active risk being knocked out of combat and recover lost Endurance more slowly (see Resting).

### Die Roll Results table (p.11)
| Feat Die | Result | Special |
|---|---|---|
| 1–10 | Read number normally | — |
| Gandalf rune | Success, regardless of TN | — |
| Eye of Sauron | Counts as a zero | — |

| Success Dice | Result | Special |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Read number normally | If Weary: counts as a 0 |
| 4–5 | Read number normally | — |
| ℧ (6 w/ Success icon) | Read number normally | On a successful roll, one or more ℧ indicates higher quality of success |

### Detailed Die Roll Procedure
1. Define what the Player-hero is trying to achieve — determines consequences of success/failure.
2. Select the ability to use (players may suggest); difficulty = the relevant Attribute TN.
3. Take one Feat Die and Success Dice equal to the ability rating (two Feat Dice if Favoured/Ill-Favoured).
4. Gain/lose dice per bonuses/penalties.
5. Make the roll. If Favoured, choose the best Feat Die result; if Ill-Favoured, the worst. A Gandalf rune is an automatic success.
6. Add up all numerical results. If Weary, ignore outlined (1/2/3) Success Dice results. Total ≥ TN → success.
7. If successful, the number of ℧ results indicates degree of success.

## CHARACTERISTICS (pages 12–18)

### Attributes
Three Attributes: **STRENGTH**, **HEART**, **WITS** — physical, emotional, and mental capabilities respectively.
- High STRENGTH: tough, fit, quick/alert, physically attractive/imposing.
- High HEART: fiery, energetic, hard to daunt; passionate/dynamic temper.
- High WITS: clever, attentive, ingenious; sharp-witted, sensible, wise.

**Target Numbers:** Each Attribute rating determines a standard TN (20 minus the Attribute score).
- STRENGTH TN — used with all STRENGTH Skills and to resolve attack rolls.
- HEART TN — used with all HEART Skills and Valour rolls (e.g., resisting fear).
- WITS TN — used with all WITS Skills and Wisdom rolls (e.g., resisting temptation of treasure).

### Distinctive Features
Traits describing an adventurer's build, temper, personality, or physical peculiarities — help players picture characters and encourage roleplaying. A Distinctive Feature can be invoked to improve a Skill roll's chances if plausible given its description; doing so makes the Player-hero **Inspired**.

### Skills
18 Skills, resolved using a Skill rating (diamond-shaped boxes on the sheet — higher = better).

**Favoured Skills:** marked with a checked box to the left of the name; all rolls with a Favoured Skill are Favoured rolls.

**Skill Categories** (organised on the sheet in three columns under each Attribute box):
- **STRENGTH Skills:** AWE, SONG, ATHLETICS, AWARENESS, HUNTING, CRAFT.
- **HEART Skills:** ENHEARTEN, BATTLE, TRAVEL, COURTESY, INSIGHT, HEALING.
- **WITS Skills:** PERSUADE, STEALTH, SCAN, EXPLORE, RIDDLE, LORE.

(Note: the text groups example skills per Attribute descriptively rather than as a strict single list — see full alphabetical Skills list below for the canonical 18.)

### Combat Proficiencies
Express how well Player-heroes fight. Resolved like Skill rolls: one Feat Die + Success Dice equal to the Proficiency rank (roll only a Feat die at rank 0). Rolled against the STRENGTH TN when making Attack rolls.

### Endurance, Hope and Parry
- **Endurance** — lost through strenuous activity/harm; heroes become Weary at/below Load.
- **Hope** — spent voluntarily to exceed limits.
- **Parry** — sets the Target Number for attack rolls targeting a Player-hero.

## SKILLS — full alphabetical list with descriptions (pages 14–15)

- **ATHLETICS** — broad Skill covering physical activities: running, leaping, climbing, swimming.
- **AWARENESS** — readiness to react/notice something unexpected, out of the ordinary, or hard to detect; reflects keen senses/experience of what is seen or heard.
- **AWE** — capacity to evoke respect in onlookers; the impression Player-heroes make on first meeting someone. Can instil wonderment, admiration, or fear. Arises from native charisma or dramatic entrance/impressive attire.
- **BATTLE** — grasp of the rules of battle and capability to manoeuvre appropriately in a violent confrontation; can gain an advantage fighting a group of foes.
- **COURTESY** — recognising common norms of decency and ancient conventions of behaviour among the Free Peoples; establishes friendly footing even with strangers.
- **CRAFT** — talent for making or mending things by hand (not the full range of smiths/wrights, but general repair/construction) — e.g., repairing a cart wheel, building a raft, starting a fire.
- **ENHEARTEN** — instilling positive feelings in others via example, charisma, and personal conviction (rather than words, which is PERSUADE); effective on individuals but particularly on crowds.
- **EXPLORE** — moving through unfamiliar Wild areas; used during a journey to find where the Company is heading, get back on track after a detour, cope with natural hazards, create paths through wilderness, or choose a campsite.
- **HEALING** — knowledge to relieve pain and apply remedies; treatments differ by culture but agree on treating serious injuries immediately; includes bone setting and use of herbs/salves.
- **HUNTING** — pursuing a creature through wild areas, following tracks, identifying quarry by spoor; also covers preparing traps and training/using hunting dogs or birds.
- **INSIGHT** — seeing beyond appearances to recognise hidden thoughts/beliefs; doesn't reveal lies directly but yields info to draw conclusions about motives.
- **LORE** — a love for learning: fascination with distant lands or family genealogy; required for actions involving knowledge. Player-heroes are assumed knowledgeable about their own people's traditions (LM should rarely require a roll for that).
- **PERSUADE** — applying reasoning to convince others of an idea/course of action; can influence small groups in appropriate contexts (e.g., a common hall); requires more time than other Personality Skills but has more lasting impact.
- **RIDDLE** — drawing conclusions from seemingly unconnected scraps of information via deduction, reasoning, intuition; used when adventurers talk about a subject but conceal part of what they know (e.g., without revealing identity). Speaking in riddles is a customary way for strangers to speak guardedly; also used to gain insight from a spoken/written riddle.
- **SCAN** — examining something closely/attentively: skim a book for relevant info, look for concealed doors/hidden inscriptions, recognise a familiar face in a crowd, locate tracks. SCAN rolls generally initiated by players; AWARENESS (not SCAN) is used to see if a hero passively notices something. One roll per inspection of a small area (e.g., a room).
- **SONG** — reciting poems, singing songs, playing instruments suitable to a character's Culture; also used to learn new works or create original compositions. All Middle-earth peoples (even Goblins/Orcs) celebrate through music/song.
- **STEALTH** — acting furtively/secretly: hiding, moving quietly, shadowing others; relies on quickness and precision, combining practised caution with judging the right moment to act.
- **TRAVEL** — in the Third Age, settlements are separated by leagues of wild/deserted areas; a TRAVEL roll may estimate journey length, read a map, or evaluate whether a stranger on the road can be approached safely.

## COMBAT PROFICIENCIES (page 17)

Four Combat Proficiencies: **AXES**, **BOWS**, **SPEARS**, **SWORDS**. Each represents familiarity with a family of similar weapons.

- **AXES** — allows use of an axe, a great axe, a long-hafted axe, or a mattock. Historically the weapon of choice for most Dwarves, surpassing the sword in nobility/respect for their folk.
- **BOWS** — enables ranged attacks using a bow or a great bow. Archers usually carry another weapon for close range, as bows and shields aren't used together.
- **SPEARS** — allows ranged and close combat attacks with a short spear or a spear, or close combat with a great spear. Warriors with a spear typically use a shield plus a secondary weapon (sword/axe).
- **SWORDS** — enables close combat attacks using short swords, swords, and long swords. The sword is a mark of nobility among free Men and Elves; some carry curses/spells; many evil creatures make crude counterfeit swords.

**Sidebar — Brawling Attacks:** Adventurers forced to fight unarmed, with a simple dagger, or a thrown stone rely on martial training.
- Attacking unarmed or with an improvised weapon: roll dice equal to the highest Combat Proficiency, but *lose (1d)*.
- Unarmed attacks have Damage rating 1 and cannot cause a Piercing Blow.

## ENDURANCE AND HOPE (page 18)

Endurance and Hope keep Player-heroes going. Endurance is lost through exertion/harm; Hope is spent voluntarily against difficult odds. Maximum values shown on character sheets; Current boxes track ongoing changes (or use counters/dice/beads).

### Endurance
Represents resistance; lost when subjected to harm (combat damage) or strenuous tasks.
- Endurance at zero → drop unconscious, wake up after one hour with 1 Endurance point (unless also Wounded).

**Weary:** Load affects performance — heroes become Weary if Current Endurance ≤ total Load. Check the Weary box; remain Weary until Endurance rises above Load again.

**Resting:**
- **Short Rest** — up to one per day (~1 hour minimum), usually midday: recover Endurance points equal to STRENGTH (Wounded heroes recover none).
- **Prolonged Rest** — one per day normally (usually a night's sleep); the Loremaster may allow more if resting somewhere safe/comfortable. Recovers all lost Endurance (Wounded heroes recover a number of points equal to STRENGTH only).

### Hope
Spiritual fortitude/positivity reserve — lets a hopeful character keep going when physically stronger heroes despair. Players spend Hope to gain a Hope bonus on any roll, or trigger Cultural Virtues.
- Hope at zero → spiritually drained; cannot push themselves beyond their limits (cannot spend Hope).

**Recovering Hope:**
- Player-heroes at zero Hope recover a single point once they take a Prolonged Rest.
- All Player-heroes recover a number of Hope points equal to their HEART score between adventures.

## VALOUR AND WISDOM (page 19)

Two gauges measuring heroic stature:
- **WISDOM** — trust in one's own capabilities, self-confidence, capacity for good judgement.
- **VALOUR** — a measure of courage, tempered by dangerous deeds; willingness to place oneself in danger for others' safety.

The Loremaster may require Valour or Wisdom rolls, resolved as usual (one Feat die + Success Dice equal to the rating of the challenged ability). Difficulty is the HEART TN for Valour rolls, and the WITS TN for Wisdom rolls.

### Rewards
Characteristics of high-quality weapons/defensive gear given to heroes by their family/folk, or by generous lords letting them choose from a personal armoury. Rewards for the Pregenerated Heroes are explained on their character sheets.

### Virtues
Special abilities complementing Skills/Combat Proficiencies — aptitudes that come naturally or arise through practice; more than simple talents, they define a hero's heroic stature. Virtues practiced by the Pregenerated Heroes are explained on their character sheets.

### Useful Items
Any tool/instrument/device carried to perform specific tasks. Listed on a character sheet, associated with a Skill. If a roll outside combat uses a Skill associated with a useful item and the Loremaster deems it should grant an advantage, the Player-hero *gains (1d)*. Only one item can benefit the same die roll.

## ADVENTURING (pages 20–21)

*The One Ring* is played as a series of sessions (like TV episodes); 2–3 sessions typically form a longer "adventure." Each session, the Loremaster challenges players with difficult circumstances, riddles, and opponents. Gameplay is a mix of preparation and improvisation.

**Sidebar — The Adventures:** The five ready-to-play scenarios in "The Adventures" booklet were created for this Starter Set. Playable with the full *The One Ring* rules too, but written with the Pregenerated characters in mind.

### How Adventures Work
An average adventure occupies 2–3 sessions of ~3 hours each. Every session tends to follow the same structure:
1. **Introduction**
2. **Scenes**
3. **End of the Session**

**1. Introduction:** If the first session of an adventure, the Loremaster presents the current situation: a date/time of year (*when*), a location (*where*), and an introductory situation (*what*) including info allowing the Company's involvement (*why*). If not the first session, the opening scene picks up where the previous session left off.

**2. Scenes:** Gameplay transitions from scene to scene, each requiring meaningful decisions. Scenes vary from under an hour (short encounter) to the entire session (a major gathering). Between main and secondary scenes, a session should not exceed 3–4 hours.

**3. End of a Session:** Two possibilities when time is up:
- If the session ends without a satisfactory conclusion, play resumes later with the Loremaster summarising what happened previously.
- If the current narrative arc concludes, the Loremaster draws the final curtain — the adventure is over; time to plan for the next.

## COMBAT (pages 22–24)

Even in a peaceful land like the Shire, combat may be the sad but unavoidable culmination of a scene.

**Sidebar — Stance Cards:** The Starter Set box includes stance cards meant for use with the full *The One Ring* core rulebook — full rules for fighting/travelling let players choose a more specific stance and assign a role during a journey. Players wishing to use Stance cards can, knowing that *Forward*, *Open*, and *Defensive* are all Close Combat stances, and *Rearward* is the Ranged Combat stance.

### Opening Volleys
If combatants start at a distance, all are allowed ranged attacks before close-quarters combat begins. The Loremaster determines how many volleys based on distance (at least one volley with a bow/thrown weapon under most circumstances; more if the distance is greater). Volley attacks are resolved as normal ranged attacks; normally Player-heroes launch first unless circumstances favour the opposition. After opening volleys, combatants close the distance and fight at close quarters.

### Close Quarters Rounds
Once close-quarters combat begins, play proceeds in rounds:
1. **Stance:** Player-heroes choose Close Combat or Ranged Combat at the start of each round. Player-heroes can freely choose Close Combat; they may only choose Ranged Combat if at least two other adventurers are fighting in Close Combat.
   - Close Combat fighters exchange blows with swords/axes/close weapons; Ranged Combat fighters stay back using bows/spears-as-ranged and can only be targeted by attackers using similar ranged weapons.
2. **Engagement:** All combatants in Close Combat are paired with one or more opponents. Player-heroes without an adversary must join another Player-hero fighting an enemy. Ranged Combat Player-heroes cannot be engaged in Close Combat and can only be attacked by enemies using ranged weapons.
3. **Attack Rolls Resolution:** All combatants' actions are resolved, close combat first, then ranged.

**Sidebar — More Enemies Than Heroes:** If there are more enemies than heroes engaged, the Loremaster chooses whether "spare" enemies engage a Player-hero already fighting, or stand back to attack with a ranged weapon (which may then target any Player-hero involved in the fight).

### Attack Rolls Resolution (detail)
Unless specified otherwise, Player-heroes' attacks resolve first; then Loremaster adversaries'. Close Combat rolls resolve before Ranged Attack rolls. An attack is a die roll using the Combat Proficiency for the weapon.
- Player-hero attack difficulty = their STRENGTH TN, modified by the Parry rating of the targeted enemy.
- Adversary attack difficulty against Player-heroes = the target hero's Parry score.

A successful attack inflicts damage as **Endurance Loss** based on the weapon used, and may inflict additional **Special Damage** based on Degree of Success. Attacks may cause longer-lasting injuries if a **Piercing Blow** is scored.

### Endurance Loss
On a successful melee/ranged attack, the target immediately loses Endurance equal to the Damage rating of the weapon used.
- Player-heroes whose Endurance drops to/below total Load become Weary; at zero Endurance, they drop unconscious.
- Adversaries do not normally become Weary from Endurance loss, but are normally eliminated on reaching zero Endurance.

### Special Damage
A successful attack roll producing one or more Success icons (℧) can trigger special results (multiple icons can trigger different results, or the same result multiple times):
- **HEAVY BLOW — any weapon (℧):** Spend 1 Success icon to inflict an additional Endurance loss on your adversary equal to your STRENGTH rating.
- **FEND OFF — any close combat weapon (℧):** Spend 1 Success icon to gain a Parry modifier of +2 against the next attack aimed at you.
- **PIERCE — Bows and Spears (℧), Swords (℧℧):** Spend 1 (Bows/Spears) or 2 (Swords) Success icons to modify a Feat die numerical result of your attack by +2, possibly triggering a Piercing Blow (Eye of Sauron and Gandalf rune results are unaffected).

### Piercing Blows
In addition to Endurance loss, any successful attack may inflict a Piercing Blow.
- An attack roll produces a Piercing Blow on a 10 or Gandalf rune result on the Feat Die.
- Characters hit by a Piercing Blow must immediately roll one Feat Die plus Success Dice equal to the **PROTECTION** value of the armour worn (a **Protection Test**).
- The Target Number for this roll = the Injury rating of the attacker's weapon. Failure → the target receives a life-threatening blow — a **Wound**.

### Wounds
Most adversaries are killed outright when Wounded (unless specified otherwise in their description). Player-heroes can resist being Wounded once without serious consequences, but risk more if injured a second time.
- First Wound: check the Wounded box on the character sheet. Wounded heroes recover Endurance slowly (see Resting).
- Second Wound (while Wounded box already checked): Endurance drops to zero and the hero falls unconscious. This second Wound is not recorded on the sheet.

Player-heroes recover from injuries between adventures, or in a week. A HEALING roll can reduce recovery time by 1 day, plus 1 more day per ℧ icon scored (minimum 1 day). Each hero may receive a successful HEALING roll only once; a failed roll cannot be repeated until at least a day has passed.

**Sidebar — Serious Injuries and Death:** The injury rules in this Starter Set are meant to evoke a mood appropriate to the included Adventures — adventuring in the Shire is meant to be exciting, maybe thrilling, but never life-threatening. Hobbit adventurers are meant to return home with an exciting tale, not to be mourned.

### Other Actions Than Attacks
Players may propose actions other than attacks (a sign of engagement, to be encouraged). Consider time, difficulty, consequences:
- Can the action be done in one round, or does it take more time? (LM decides; a round is a maximum of ~30 seconds for such purposes.)
- Does it require a roll? If so, success/failure must have clear consequences — generally, success should grant an advantage of importance equal to the disadvantage a failure would cause.

**Sidebar — Talking with the Enemy:** The Company often faces creatures capable of speech (Orcs and the most intelligent Trolls speak a debased Common Tongue; Dragons can outwit the smartest adventurer in conversation). Good roleplaying/clever thinking might let heroes trick a powerful enemy (e.g., tricking Stone-Trolls, Bilbo's Riddle Game with Gollum, entertaining Smaug) — but a cunning adversary might do the same to the heroes.

## Notes on differences vs. the Core Rules

This Starter Set booklet is an explicitly *condensed* version of the full *The One Ring* 2e rules, intended for new/younger players and limited to Shire-based adventuring. Differences/simplifications observed:

- **No detailed Stance system:** the full core rules have a richer stance/role system for combat and travel (Forward/Open/Defensive/Rearward stances plus travel roles); this booklet only briefly gestures at Stance Cards as an optional add-on tied to the core rulebook, without detailing the mechanical effects of each stance beyond labelling two as Close Combat vs Ranged Combat categories.
- **No Journey/Travel mini-game detail:** Travel is treated simply via the TRAVEL and EXPLORE Skills; the fuller journey-role/journey-event structure from the core rules is not reproduced here.
- **Injury/death tuned down:** explicitly states the tone is "never life-threatening" for this Starter Set, with a sidebar acknowledging this is a deliberate softening compared to the presumably harsher default assumption in the full game.
- **Character creation is not included at all** in this booklet — it only uses ready-made Pregenerated Heroes; full character-creation rules (Culture, Calling, Attribute distribution, etc.) are assumed to live in the Core Rules and are not reproduced here.
- **Combat Proficiencies simplified to 4 categories** (Axes, Bows, Spears, Swords) — consistent presentation with the core rules' four Proficiencies, but explained here in brief, weapon-list form rather than full weapon-table detail (no weapon stat tables reproduced — those live on the character/Item Cards instead).
- **Skills list presented via two overlapping groupings** — the Characteristics chapter (p.13) groups example Skills descriptively under STRENGTH/HEART/WITS headers, while the dedicated Skills chapter (pp.14–15) gives the full alphabetical list of (18) Skills; both are reproduced above for cross-reference.
